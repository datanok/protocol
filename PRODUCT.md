# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Self-optimizers: people who treat personal development as a system. Athletes tracking volume, learners building skills deliberately, builders running their days with intention. They open Protocol to log what they did and check what comes next — not to explore, not to customize, not to be entertained. The session is the goal; Protocol is the ledger.

## Product Purpose

Protocol is a personal life OS: a single structured system for tracking fitness, skill-building, study, and nutrition. It exists because no existing tool treats all four domains with equal seriousness. Success looks like a daily commit that takes under 3 minutes, data that accumulates into visible progress, and a plan that earns trust by staying accurate and opinionated.

## Brand Personality

Disciplined. Deliberate. Precise.

The tone is a training log written by someone who takes their craft seriously — no hype, no streak-shaming, no social layer. Functional without being cold. Structured without being bureaucratic.

## Anti-references

- **Linear / Raycast**: Too cold and too keyboard-centric — feels like dev tooling, not a personal OS. Protocol should feel closer to a well-made physical journal than an IDE.
- **MyFitnessPal / Strava**: Feature-bloated, corporate UX, mass-market trade-offs. Protocol should feel personal and opinionated, not built for everyone.
- **BeReal / fitness social apps**: Gamified streaks as primary motivation, social proof loops, neon palettes, rounded everything. Protocol is not a game.

## Design Principles

1. **The commit is the ceremony.** The daily log is the emotional center of the product. It should feel deliberate and satisfying to complete — not like a form.
2. **Data over decoration.** Every visual element either carries information or provides structure. Nothing decorates for its own sake.
3. **Opinionated by default.** Protocol makes decisions for the user (today's exercises, the active plan, default durations). It does not ask unnecessary questions.
4. **Progress is earned, not performed.** No public achievements, no streak flames, no confetti. Accountability comes from the data.
5. **Consistent vocabulary, no surprises.** Same patterns screen to screen. A button that saves always looks like a save button. Familiarity is a feature.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort: no formal WCAG target. Focus on keyboard navigability for power users, visible focus states, and sufficient contrast in both light and dark modes. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning for critical states.
